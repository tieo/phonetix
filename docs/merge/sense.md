# What does not make sense, read as a stranger would read it

Written 2026-09-13 from a cleared install of both surfaces: the extension's popup with no
settings at all, and the app's own screen after `pm clear`. Every line below is something a
person meets before they have read any documentation, because that is the only state that
matters for whether a product makes sense.

The features work. The product around them does not introduce itself, does not say what to do
first, and offers controls for things the reader cannot use yet.

## Both surfaces

1. **A fresh install cannot answer a single word, and neither surface says so.** Everything
   depends on two settings: the language the reader reads into, and where dictionaries come
   from. Both are buried below controls that do nothing until they are set, and the dictionary
   host is an empty box with a `https://…` placeholder. A reader who does not already know the
   address of a pack host has no way forward and is not told that.

2. **The order is the order the code grew in, not the order a reader decides in.** "How often"
   - a bar, the most prominent control on both surfaces - is above both of the settings that
   make anything happen at all. What a reader decides, in order, is: what am I reading into,
   where do the dictionaries come from, what should appear over a word, how much of the page.

## The extension

3. **"Not sure what this page is" opens "How it reads".** The row names a problem with this
   page and leads to a screen about transcription detail, stress marks and card timing.

4. **The first line under the product's name is about an edge case**: "the default is what
   happens on sites you have not decided about". It is the first sentence a reader reads, and
   it is about per-site exceptions they have not made yet.

5. **"The word for something" says "Choose a language to read into first" and does not take
   you there.** A dead end that names its own cure.

## The phone

6. **The accents card lists every language the tables know**, each with its own row of chips,
   on a device holding no dictionary at all. The extension shows the accent for the language on
   the page and folds the rest away; the phone shows English, French, German, Portuguese,
   Spanish and more, always.

7. **"The mark" has one switch and two rows that look like rows and are not.** "Hold it, then
   sweep" and "Press it" are prose in the shape of a control; a reader taps them and nothing
   happens.

8. **"Words are not touchable" is a heading that renames itself when the switch is flipped**,
   and is written as a double negative.

9. **The app introduces itself as the old product**: "Pronunciation, over the words you read".
   What it draws by default is what a word means.

10. **The preview previews nothing.** Under "How often" sits a sample paragraph with no
    annotations on it, because nothing can be annotated until the two settings above are made.

## What the fix is

Not more features. In order:

- One block at the top of each surface that says what is missing and does the next step, and
  nothing below it that pretends to work before it does.
- The settings ordered the way a reader decides: into what, from where, what appears, how much.
- Every row leads to the thing it names.
- A setting appears when there is something for it to change.
